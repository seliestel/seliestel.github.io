*Literature Unbound* offers a concise introduction to literary studies in the twenty-first century, bringing together its core disciplines: literary theory, literary criticism, and comparative literature. 

Instead of surveying schools and movements chronologically, the book is organised around the concepts and questions that shape current research, from meaning, ideology, and identity to affect, care, and the relations between humans and nonhumans. It explains the main reading methods practised by critics today, including close, distant, experiential, affordance, dialectical, and reparative reading. Finally, it explores how comparatists engage with world literature, translation, cultural encounters, intermediality, and literature beyond the human. 

At a time of deep technological transformation and ecological crisis, the book argues that literary studies, to remain relevant, needs to cross the disciplinary, linguistic, cultural, even biological boundaries that still constrain literature. Written in a clear, essayistic style, it will appeal to graduate and advanced undergraduate students, teachers, and general readers.
